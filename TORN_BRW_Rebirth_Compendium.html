Reference Section

Quick Game Loop

Each game turn runs through six phases:

1. Black Rose
2. Study
3. Preparation
4. Action
5. Evocation
6. Clean-up

The core loop is: draw spells, program them onto the Mage Sheet, activate mages and spells, activate evocations, then clean up and check endgame. TORN repeatedly emphasizes that the game flow itself is not hard; the real complexity is in spell text, timing, targeting, and school-specific mechanics.

Reference Section

Phase Reference

1. Black Rose Phase

  • Crown holder becomes first player for the turn/phase.
  • Shift events on the Event Track; discard the event in the final/third position.
  • Draw, reveal, and place a new event; instant events resolve and discard immediately.
  • Resolve Black Rose events from left to right.
  • Award Black Rose points when an event is revealed or discarded if the card indicates points.
  • Players may optionally discard one active quest; this gives Black Rose points based on moon/quest level.
  • Any player without an active quest draws a quest of the current moon.
  • Discard active/completed quests down to quest limit only when checked in this phase.

Important clarifications:

  • Black Rose gains quest points only when players voluntarily discard active quests in this phase, not when discarding because of quest limit.
  • If Black Rose draws a quest by effect, it gains points: 1/2/3 for first/second/third moon.
  • Only quests that grant Black Rose points go to the Event Board quest slot.

2. Study Phase

  • Draw 2 cards from your Grimoire into hand; if the Grimoire is empty, shuffle Memories into a new Grimoire.
  • Draw/look at 4 cards from any allowed school decks in the Library, keep 2, and discard the other 2 to the relevant school discard piles.
  • You may discard one single spell from hand to the Library during this phase; custom spells are set aside instead.
  • Discard down to hand-size limit only at the end of Study Phase.

Common mistake prevention:

  • Do not mix your hand, Grimoire, Memories, and the 4 Library cards. The 2 discarded Library cards must come from the 4 drawn Library cards, not from your hand.
  • As a beginner house rule, TORN suggests replacing "draw 4, keep 2" with "draw 2 from your school and keep both" for the first game.
  • Default draw source is your Grimoire unless an effect says otherwise.
  • Default spell discard destination is Memories unless an effect says otherwise.
  • Forgotten spell deck is not a school deck; draw from it only when explicitly instructed.

3. Preparation Phase

  • Place 2-4 spell cards face-down onto your Mage Sheet.
  • Typically this means 1 Quick Spell and 1-3 Standard Spells.
  • Prepared spells are ready.
  • When a prepared card is later revealed, the face-down orientation determines which half/effect resolves; you do not choose the side at cast time.
  • If a spell is played to the Mage Sheet later by an effect, it goes into one of the slots and may replace a spell.

4. Action Phase

  • Resolve Action events from left to right.
  • In turn order, each mage takes repeated activations until all pass.
  • In each activation, a mage must take 1 or 2 available actions; pass only if no mandatory/available action remains.
  • Available options include physical action, casting a Standard Spell, casting a Quick Spell, or discarding a ready spell to move 0-1 room via momentum.
  • Standard Spells must be cast in order from slot I to III.
  • You cannot cast two Standard Spells in one activation.
  • Completed quests may be solved during your activation before or after an action, not during an action.
  • If in your cell, your next action must move you out of the cell; discarding an activated spell to move is optional only where allowed.

Action reminders:

  • Attacks/spells without valid targets still occur but may have no effect.
  • Physical actions are not dummy-target actions; punching the air is not an option.
  • Activated Trap and Protection spells remain active until triggered or removed, even after passing.
  • When triggers are ambiguous, resolve clockwise starting with the player next to the triggering player.

5. Evocation Phase

  • Black Rose activates all Nightmares/Umbras first.
  • Then players and Avatars activate their evocations in turn order.
  • An evocation may move and attack, before or after moving, and may use its ability if available.
  • Avatar-owned evocations activate in player order because Avatar counts as a mage.

Nightmare/Umbras:

  • Umbras are Nightmare archetype evocations.
  • They are assigned to a mage, not summoned.
  • Max 1 Nightmare per evocation slot.
  • They are owned by Black Rose unless an effect temporarily changes control.
  • They move with the haunted mage/avatar and are removed when that mage/avatar is defeated.
  • Spell effects cannot move Nightmares.

6. Clean-up Phase

  • Optionally take activated traps and protection spells back to hand if allowed by the edition/reference.
  • Return still-activated traps to owners' hands in classic reference.
  • Remove used Forgotten Spells from the game.
  • Discard other used spells from Mage Sheet to Memories.
  • Flip Physical Action tokens back to available.
  • Flip/reset Room Activation tokens.
  • For Avatar: add one cube to one jinx, remove one Nightmare assigned to Avatar if available, then shuffle as instructed.
  • Resolve full instability tracks: rebuild/destroy rooms and award room points/tokens.
  • Check game end at the end of cleanup: default threshold is 30 Power Points/Black Rose moon cube.
Reference Section

Spell Lifecycle

General spell flow:

  1. Spell card in hand.
  2. Prepare it onto Mage Sheet face-down.
  3. Spell is ready.
  4. Cast combat/contingency spell or cast/activate trap/protection spell.
  5. Trap/protection may become activated and wait for a trigger.
  6. If trigger is accepted, reveal and resolve.
  7. Used spell goes to Memories, or Forgotten Spell is removed from game during cleanup.

Key spell timing clarifications:

  • When a spell is revealed, first check reveal triggers, then immediately place the middle instability icon if present.
  • Then check other triggers, choose/announce target, check target-related triggers, and resolve the spell effect.
  • A spell has one effect (or reverse effect), usually written as a series of sentences.
  • Resolve as much of an effect as possible, sentence by sentence.
  • If a condition/cost/enhancement requirement is not met, skip the entire conditional part.
  • If an effect is interrupted by defeat, resolve up to the end of the current sentence, handle defeat, then continue with the next sentence of the same effect unless the rules stop the action.

Targeting, Range, and Line of Sight

  • Target icons define eligible targets: self, mage-not-self, model-not-self, evocation, room/area, special, everywhere.
  • Mage/model target icons exclude your own mage unless explicitly self-targeting.
  • You cannot target mages in their cells.
  • A number on a target/room/model icon is range, not exact distance.
  • Range N includes range 0 through N unless the effect says otherwise.
  • Room(1) includes the target room itself and adjacent rooms; "adjacent room" excludes range 0.
  • Asterisk/everywhere means any range and no line-of-sight requirement.
  • Line of sight is straight through door centers/intermediate room centers, like a star radiating from the caster.
  • Target is chosen after reveal and instability placement. Once validly chosen, it remains the target during effect resolution even if later moved out of range/LOS, unless it enters a cell.
  • Special/star target means the card text explains targeting.

Cards, Decks, and Discards

  • Draw Card: if no deck is specified, draw from your Grimoire.
  • Event cards with crown symbol give the drawer the Crown Token, but first-player change waits until the next phase.
  • Discard Card: destination depends on card type/effect; spell cards usually go to Memories.
  • Forgotten Spells never go to Memories. If they would be discarded from hand or Mage Sheet to Memories or elsewhere, remove them from the game.
  • Selected supply cards such as evocation, haunting, or jinx cards usually return to supply after use/removal.
  • Search Deck: if no shuffle is specified after searching, no shuffle is needed.
  • Empty deck: shuffle that deck's discard pile to form a new deck.
  • Hand size is checked only at the end of Study Phase.
  • Quest limit is checked only during Black Rose Phase.

Damage, Healing, Conversion, and Immunity

  • Inflict X Damage: controller places their damage markers on the target model/Mage/Avatar/evocation.
  • Suffer X Damage: damage is inflicted on your Mage/Avatar by the source specified.
  • Pain X: Black Rose inflicts X damage on you.
  • A model cannot take more damage markers than its health value.
  • A model is defeated when damage reaches health.
  • Healing removes damage markers from target; players choose which markers, while Black Rose/Avatar handling often proceeds right-to-left.
  • Conversion replaces up to X damage or instability markers with the converter's markers; replaced markers return to owners.
  • A mage and their evocations are immune to damage inflicted or converted by that mage.
  • Your spells/effects cannot damage your own miniatures.
  • Avatar and Avatar-owned evocations are immune to Black Rose damage.
  • Evocation damage is not mage damage and does not trigger traps requiring mage-inflicted damage.
Reference Section

Defeat and Trophies

  • A defeated Mage/Avatar goes to its cell.
  • Final blow gets a trophy.
  • Damage contributors receive points by ranking: top/second/other with at least 1 damage, with ties reduced by 1 but never below 1.
  • Do not gain points for your own defeat.
  • Assigned Nightmares are removed when their haunted Mage/Avatar is defeated.
  • Summoned evocations remain in play when the Mage is defeated.
  • A spell just cast or an activation may continue in the chamber after defeat; physical actions are canceled/stop remaining consecutive effects.
  • Defeat itself can be interrupted by protection/healing effects that prevent defeat.
Reference Section

Quests

  • A drawn and kept quest becomes active and is placed face-down on the active quest side of the Mage Sheet.
  • When its task is first fulfilled, reveal it.
  • If cube slots are present, repetitions are tracked with cubes; otherwise one fulfillment completes it.
  • When completed, return quest cubes to reserve and move it face-up to the completed side.
  • During your activation, solve one or more completed quests before/after an action.
  • Solving gives the lower quest effect/bonus and Power Points, then the quest flips face-down as solved.
  • Effects that solve active/incomplete quests give only Power Points, not the bonus effect.
  • Active and completed quest limits are separate when checked.
  • Beginner/strategy note: quests are one of the main scoring paths, alongside damage/defeats and room instability.
Reference Section

Rooms and Instability

  • Many spells or command cards place instability/stability immediately when revealed or resolved.
  • Rooms cannot hold more markers than their track has slots.
  • If a room's instability track fills, resolve rebuild/destroy during cleanup.
  • Ranking for room points follows the same minimum-1 rule when eligible and tied.
  • Room activation: first check the room is available or that the effect permits activating an unavailable room.
  • The room is activated before resolving its effect; triggers for room activation happen at that point.
  • Pay costs if required, then resolve the room effect if paid.
  • Flip the activation token to used/inactive after activation.
  • You can activate a room even if you cannot pay for or resolve its effect, except the Black Rose Room requires full cost payment.
  • Black Rose Room cost is paid by discarding cards from hand, not Mage Sheet.
Reference Section

Evocations, Summons, Upgrades, and Jinxes

  • Summon by name or archetype: take the matching evocation card and model, assign it to an empty evocation slot, and place the model in the target room.
  • If no empty slot exists, a Mage may discard an existing evocation first.
  • A Mage can control only one large evocation at a time in Rebirth.
  • Remove effects from school spells generally affect only non-Forgotten evocations with printed health 3 or less; damage does not reduce printed health.
  • Removing your own evocation ignores that health limit.
  • Forgotten Spell remove effects can remove any evocation unless otherwise stated.
  • Evocations can have only one upgrade. A later upgrade requires discarding one of the two.
  • Jinx cards remain after defeat and describe their own discard conditions.
  • If a Mage would receive a jinx already present, or the jinx card is unavailable, ignore it.
  • Teleport/place effects enter only the destination room; intermediate rooms are not entered and do not trigger traps.
  • Movement enters each room along the way, but not the starting room.
Reference Section

Crown, First Player, and Ties

  • Crown holder becomes first player at the beginning of every phase, not just every game turn.
  • Drawing/revealing an event with a crown symbol grants the Crown Token.
  • Crown holder gains +1 point at end scoring.
  • Ties are generally broken by most solved quests, then most trophies, then Crown/first player decides.
  • Ranking awards with ties subtract 1 point, minimum 1, if the player qualifies for the rank.
Reference Section

Endgame and Scoring

  • Game end checks at the end of Clean-up Phase.
  • Default trigger: a player or Black Rose reaches/surpasses 30 Power Points or the Black Rose Moon Cube threshold.
  • Rebirth/Avatar reference: players, Black Rose, and Avatar may score endgame bonuses.
  • Classic reference: Black Rose does not take part in endgame bonus scoring.
  • Bonus categories include number of quests, number of trophies, room activation tokens where applicable, and Crown +1.
  • Most Power Points wins.

Teaching the Game

TORN's teaching approach is to keep the first explanation to about 15 minutes and teach through the first turn rather than front-loading every detail.

Recommended flow:

  1. Explain the loop: draw spells, program spells, act, activate minions, clean up.
  2. Show the quick turn aid and point to the six phases.
  3. Explain how to score: rebuild/destroy rooms, defeat mages, solve quests, gain/use Forgotten Spells, plus endgame quests/trophies/crown.
  4. Walk through sample spells to explain spell type, target, range, trap/protection, and effects.
  5. Play phases 1-3 with guidance.
  6. During Action Phase, explain activations, physical actions, spell order, and evocations as they arise.
  7. Teach cleanup by doing it.

Teaching advice:

  • Tell new players the main loop is manageable and you will handle procedural upkeep.
  • Warn that spell reading and spell icons are the main challenge.
  • Encourage players to focus on one school in the first game.
  • Prepare beforehand, ideally by playing one or two solo turns.
  • Keep the first game moving; if a ruling is not fundamental, let Crown decide and clarify after the game.

Beginner Setup and School Advice

Good starting schools named by TORN:

  • Alchemy with Angela
  • Technomancy with Arianna
  • Hex with Gramigna
  • Shamanic with Sefu
  • Geomancy with Talia
  • Pestilence with Pestilentia
  • War with Bellum
  • Forge with Angela or Etna
  • Lex with Bartholomaus or Ullrich
  • Dai Jin as a flexible mage for multiple schools

General school advice:

  • Mage-school synergy matters and can have a large impact.
  • For first games, choose mages that directly support their schools.
  • Most Rebirth schools are self-contained and work best when players mostly draft from their starting school.
  • Mixing schools becomes more useful once players understand each school's engine.
  • Deadly Masks provides matching mages for the core-box schools and strengthens several strategies.

FAQ 2024 Changes Highlighted by TORN

  • Heka/Hex: clarified that jinxes are discarded, not removed, by placing enough cubes on them.
  • Oracle: after drawing events, shuffle instant events back into the event deck.
  • Nightmare: clarified Nightmares as evocations, assignment, and movement with haunted mage.
  • Forge/War: equipment can only be summoned by effects targeting yourself.
  • Famine: Avatar counts as a mage; Avatar's black cube pool must be separate from Black Rose's cube pool.
  • Druidic: heavily reworked; transformations are cards next to Mage Board, not evocation transformations. They end when replaced or when the mage is defeated. Feral Magic and Communion with Nature improve access and recovery.
  • Death: Destiny tokens resolve during defeat step 3 by owner choice, no longer automatically in cleanup. Instant-kill effects costing 3 Destiny tokens now grant an extra trophy.

Acquisition and Value Guidance

TORN's value ranking from the "what am I missing" thread:

  • A: Rebirth core box.
  • A: ROTAS, because it adds broadly useful content and many Avatars, improving 1-2 player and even 3-player games.
  • B/A situational: Deadly Masks, especially for 5-6 players and for synergy mages that unlock core schools like Alchemy/Shamanic.
  • A: Apocalypse, with 4 schools, 4 Forgotten evocations, tournament mode, and strong value.
  • B: Gaea, Forge, Jukas.
  • C: Duel Lex, Seal of Fire, some promos depending on collector interest.
  • D: Duel Void and crossover characters/content unless collecting everything.
  • E: lower-value promos/Ashes/crossover material for non-collectors.
  • Used original BRW core can be an excellent buy if layout/wording differences are acceptable.

Overall guidance: Rebirth core + ROTAS + Apocalypse is the high-value foundation. Beyond that, purchases become increasingly inefficient unless you are a collector or want specific schools/mages.

Mage Avatars Supplement

Mage Avatars are TORN's fan-made automated mages that replace missing players or add AI-controlled school personalities.

Core concept:

  • Mage Avatars represent classic Mages and matching Schools.
  • Initial set: Rikkart/Agony, Arianna/Technomancy, Baron Doria/Nightmare, Angela/Alchemy.
  • They can be used with one, several, or no real players.
  • Without real players, you act as Game Master and make Crown/Ruler decisions.
  • They can also be used alongside a regular Black Rose Avatar; then use the Avatar Event Deck.
  • Mage Avatars are not Black Rose. They are affected by Black Rose effects and compete to win.

Components and difficulty:

  • Each Mage Avatar uses a Mage Avatar Card, Rule Card, and 10-card Command Deck.
  • One 10-card deck is used across all three moon phases.
  • Default difficulty progression is 3-4-5 by moon phase.
  • Easier options: 3-4-4, 3-3-4, or 3-3-3.
  • Harder options: 4-5-5, 4-5-6, or higher.
  • Difficulty increase applies immediately at moon change and may grant another activation if final activation has not already happened.

Ruler Token automation:

  • Since Mage Avatars can hold the Crown Token, a Ruler Token is introduced.
  • Ruler Token passes clockwise to the next real player whenever Crown changes ownership.
  • If a Mage Avatar needs an ambiguous decision, the Ruler Token holder decides.
  • Otherwise, Crown works normally.

Dominion:

  • All Mage Avatars have Dominion.
  • If they would activate in a cell, they are not activated there; place them in the Black Rose Room or a Dominion-specified room type.
  • Choose the eligible room with the most instability cubes, at least 1 cube, and at least one empty instability slot.
  • If tied, choose the closest to the Black Rose Room; if still tied, Ruler decides.
  • If none qualify, place the Mage Avatar in the Black Rose Room.

Setup summary:

  • Assign each Mage Avatar a random color and matching Mage Sheet.
  • Place Mage Avatar Card in Mage Card area and Rule Card in Quick Spell area.
  • Shuffle Command Deck and place in Grimoire slot.
  • Do not include their school in the Library and ignore their Personal Spells.
  • Track activations with counters/cubes.
  • Randomly assign Crown to player or Mage Avatar; randomly assign Ruler Token to a real player.
  • Place Mage Avatar model according to Dominion.

Special handling:

  • Persistent Command Cards use Persistent Symbols I-III; place matching token on target and keep the card face-up in the corresponding Standard Spell slot.
  • Non-persistent Command Cards discard face-up to Memories.
  • Reshuffle! cards cause deck + Memories to shuffle into a new deck after resolution.
  • Only during Cleanup are all Command Cards, including persistent ones, shuffled fresh.
  • Mage Avatars do not draw quests in Black Rose Phase; if told to draw quests, they solve them immediately for points.
  • Mage Avatars can trigger traps, protection, and persistent spells.
  • If a Mage Avatar activates a room, it does so without generating an effect.
  • If an event asks a Mage Avatar to choose, it picks the first option if possible.
  • Mage Avatars can win; ties follow core rules.

High-Value Rules Checklist

  • Crown holder becomes first player at the beginning of every phase.
  • You cannot cast two Standard Spells in one activation.
  • Standard Spells cast in order I-II-III.
  • You may discard a ready spell for momentum instead of casting it.
  • Activated traps/protections are not ready and cannot be used for momentum.
  • Apply spell instability immediately when revealing the spell.
  • Hand limit checks only at end of Study Phase.
  • Quest limits check only in Black Rose Phase.
  • Forgotten Spells are removed from the game instead of going to Memories.
  • Range number means up to that range, including 0.
  • Mage/model target icons do not include yourself.
  • Your own effects cannot damage your own miniatures.
  • Evocation damage is not mage damage.
  • Jinxes stay after defeat.
  • Large evocation limit is one per Mage in Rebirth.
  • Equipment limit: you cannot own two equipments with the same name.
  • Game end triggers only at end of Cleanup.
